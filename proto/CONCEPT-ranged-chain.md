## name
Ranged Chain

## source
The anchor chain of a Coast Guard cutter, ranged on the forecastle deck for inspection. Real material logic, all of it load-bearing here: (1) Chain is measured in SHOTS of 15 fathoms. 75 Hard is 75 days; 75 fathoms is exactly five shots. One day = one fathom; with 3-inch stud-link chain a fathom is six links; the six daily tasks are the six links of today's fathom. (2) Shot markings are a real convention: the detachable link joining shots is painted red at 15 fm, white at 30, blue at 45, with turns of seizing wire on the adjacent white links (one turn for the first shot, two for the second, three for the third); on a chain this short the second-to-last shot is painted entirely yellow and the last shot entirely red so the deck crew knows the bitter end is coming. That is exactly a 5-shot chain: shots 1-3 red/white/blue, shot 4 yellow, shot 5 red. So the color of a person's links tells everyone which 15-day block of 75 Hard they are in, and days 61-75 literally show as the bitter end. (3) Ranging: when chain is inspected it is flaked out on deck in parallel rows, one shot per run, the deck crew chalking marks next to it. Five people = five runs of one continuous chain. (4) Ship time: the day is divided into watches and struck on the ship's bell in pairs, eight bells ending each watch; midnight is eight bells in the first watch. 75 Hard's day ends at exactly that. (5) Darken ship: after 2000 the weather deck goes to red lighting. (6) A parted chain, a chalked deck log time, "all hands". Uncommon because nobody makes a fitness app out of ground tackle; apt because Wade stood on that deck, and because the whole point of shot markings is to know at a glance how much chain is out and how close you are to the end.

## one_object
A single stud-link anchor chain, black iron, ranged on a haze-grey painted deck in five parallel runs joined by turns at the row ends, so it reads as one chain flaked out for inspection. Each run is one person and is six links long: today's fathom. Links that have been earned are there, heavy, in three tones of iron with the stud across the middle and every second link seen edge-on. Links not yet earned are absent; in the other four runs the deck shows only the rust ghost where the link lies, in Wade's own run the missing links are chalked outlines. At the start of each run sits the detachable marker link in its shot color with the seizing-wire turns, and next to it a chalked name and day count. At the right end of a finished run a chalked 24-hour time. Above the deck, a bulkhead strip with the date, the name of the current watch, and eight bell marks filled in pairs as they are struck. Nothing glows. Nothing is a card. The only colors on the deck are iron, chalk, rust, and paint the boatswain would actually use.

## the_move
Wade taps a chalked outline in his run. The chalk is wiped (fades in 80 ms). A link falls in from 18 px above, ease-in, 140 ms, and lands: one dry iron clank (Web Audio, two short decaying sine partials at ~2.2 kHz and ~3.4 kHz plus a 20 ms noise burst, no reverb), a 10 ms vibrate on devices that support it, and eight rust-colored dust specks kick out along the deck for 300 ms. The chain takes up the slack: every link in that run shifts 2 px toward the new link and settles back over 220 ms, which makes the whole run visibly flinch. When his sixth link lands the run comes taut: the entire run shifts 3 px right and holds, the sound is 0.8 s of chain running through a hawsepipe (filtered noise with a 9 Hz clatter envelope), and the completion time is chalked at the run's end in 24-hour ship time, "1912", hand-lettered stroke by stroke over 400 ms. When another person's link arrives via sync, the same drop happens in their run at half volume, so an open phone on the counter clanks when Mike finishes his outdoor workout. When all thirty links are in, the whole chain, all five runs, takes one taut pull together (3 px, 300 ms), one deep bell strikes, and ALL HANDS is stencilled across the bottom of the deck in white paint. Long-press a landed link for 700 ms to pull it back out: it lifts 6 px, greys, and rusts back to a ghost.

## type
Display and names: Allerta Stencil (Google Fonts, verified) for the date, watch name, column heads, names and the ALL HANDS mark, because everything painted on a cutter's deck and bulkheads goes on through a stencil. Data: Courier Prime 400/700 (verified) for day counts, completion times and the deck-log panel, because the deck log is a typed government form filled in 24-hour time. No third face; chalk lettering is the stencil face rendered with a slight roughen filter so it reads as hand-chalked rather than painted. Fallback stacks: "Allerta Stencil", "Stencil", Impact, sans-serif and "Courier Prime", "Courier New", monospace.

## tech
Plain inline SVG, CSS and vanilla JS are enough; no libraries. One SVG for the deck holding four reusable symbols: flat link (outer path and inner void using evenodd, stud rectangle, three stacked strokes for the iron tones), edge-on link, detachable marker link (with N wire-turn arcs parameterised 1-5), and the parted link. Thirty link instances are <use> elements positioned on a fixed pitch, with a CSS transform for the drop and the take-up flinch. Deck grit is an SVG feTurbulence tile of 160x160 rendered once to a data URI on load and used as a background-image, not a live filter, to keep phones cool. Rust dust is a 2D canvas overlay only alive during the 300 ms after a drop. Sounds are synthesised in Web Audio (clank, hawsepipe rattle, bell) and unlocked by the first tap on the bell marks, which also toggles sound. Bells and watches computed from Date every 30 s; USN bell convention incl. the second dog watch striking 5-8. State in localStorage under hardfive.v1 keyed by ISO date: per person {name, day, links[6], done_at, parted}. Day rollover at local midnight evaluates the day (6 links = day+1, else day=1 and parted=true). Sample data for the four others is a clock-driven schedule (e.g. MIKE diet 0700, WO1 0745, GAL 1130, PAGES 2110, WO2 1800, PHOTO 2145) so the page looks alive at any hour and links drop in on the minute if it is open. The sync seam is window.HardFive.sync = { pull(): Promise<Snapshot>, push(link): Promise<void> } with a documented Snapshot shape; today's implementation is the schedule, and the stencilled LOCAL tag becomes the connection name when a backend exists. Fonts via one Google Fonts stylesheet link; everything else inline. Verified: https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Allerta+Stencil&display=swap returned 200, and https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Courier+Prime:wght@400;700&display=swap returned 200. No cdnjs or jsdelivr script is needed.

## risks
The chain looks like clip art. Prevention: render from photographs of stud-link chain, alternate flat and edge-on links on one baseline, give each link three iron tones and a stud, and never let two flat links touch; ship it only after a screenshot at phone scale reads as iron. It collapses into a table. Prevention: the serpentine turns at the row ends and the marker links must be drawn; the moment the turns are missing it is a grid with oval checkboxes. Word creep. Prevention: the total text on screen is capped at the date, the watch name, six column heads, five names, five numbers, up to five times, LOCAL and ALL HANDS; any new label needs a removed one. The shot-color rule becomes unreadable trivia. Prevention: the marker link and the painted runs need no legend because people learn it from their own day count changing color at day 16, 31, 46, 61; the deck-log panel explains it in three lines if tapped. Darken-ship reads as the rejected dark-mode slop. Prevention: it is a single red ambient over the same haze-grey deck, only from 2000 to 0000, no glow, no gradients; the chain stays black. Sound annoys. Prevention: off until the bell marks are tapped once, remembered in localStorage. Rollover bugs at midnight and time zones. Prevention: key everything by local ISO date, evaluate on load and on a 30 s tick, and keep a dated history so a missed rollover is reconciled next open. Sample schedule leaks into real use. Prevention: the schedule lives only inside the LOCAL sync adapter and is replaced whole when a backend is plugged in.

## pitch
Today's chain, ranged on deck. One chain, five runs, one per man, six links each. Your missing links are chalked; tap one and the iron drops in and the chain takes up. Theirs drop in as they check. The marker link shows what shot of the seventy-five each of you is on; last shot is red. Eight bells, first watch, is midnight. All hands, chain's made.
